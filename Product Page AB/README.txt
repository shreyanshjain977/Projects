This project analyzes the impact of a new product page design on user conversion rates through an A/B testing experiment. Using simulated data, 5,000 users were split evenly between a control group (original page) and a treatment group (new page). The primary metric is conversion rate — the percentage of users who complete a desired action.

Historic data was simulated over 30 days with an average conversion rate of ~10%. For the experiment, users were randomly assigned to groups, and conversion rates were calculated. The treatment group showed a slightly higher conversion rate (11.6%) compared to the control (10.5%).

A Z-test was used to assess statistical significance, with a significance level (α) of 0.05 and a minimum detectable effect of 10%. The difference in conversion rates was 1.1%, but the 95% confidence interval (−0.8% to 3.0%) included zero — indicating the result is not statistically significant.

Conclusion: There is no strong evidence that the new page outperforms the current version. It's recommended to keep the existing page or run a larger experiment for more conclusive results.
